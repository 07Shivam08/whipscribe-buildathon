# Scorecard

How entries are scored, out of 100. Score yourself with `scorecard.html`
(open it in a browser, or at the GitHub Pages link in the README) and paste the
result into your introduction or pull request.

| Area | Points | What earns them |
|---|---:|---|
| **UI and UX craft** | 30 | Feels right in the hand. Empty, loading, error, done and offline states are designed. Readable on a phone. Keyboard and screen-reader reachable. A non-technical person gets it without being told. |
| **Shipped apps** | 20 | iOS or Android apps you built that are live in the App Store or Play Store, with real users. Points for: store links, ratings and reviews you handled, crash-free updates, what you learned running them in production. |
| **Building with AI, well** | 20 | You used AI tools and understood what they produced. Commit history shows judgement: good parts kept, bad parts dropped, decisions explained in the README. |
| **Finishing** | 20 | Something works end to end for a real user, rough edges handled. One finished thing beats three that nearly work. |
| **Learning in the open** | 10 | You say what was new to you, what you looked up, what went wrong and how you fixed it. |

**Reading the total:** 80 and above is a contender for the top two. 65 to 79
is strong; tell us what you would do with two more weeks. Below 50, keep
going and resubmit — early, small pull requests get feedback.

Track 1 entries (bug reports and proposals) are scored on UI and UX craft,
finishing (is the proposal a real, small, correct fix?) and learning; shipped
apps and AI use count where you show them.
